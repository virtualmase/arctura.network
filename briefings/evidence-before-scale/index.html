Before you scale an agent, scale the evidence.

A successful demonstration answers a narrow question. Expansion needs a record of the conditions, failures, and human decisions behind the result.

The source signal

NIST describes its AI Risk Management Framework as a voluntary resource for considering trustworthiness throughout the design, development, use, and evaluation of AI systems. The framework provides context for risk management; citing it is not a certification.

Source: NIST AI Risk Management Framework ↗

A demo is the beginning of a record

Arctura’s application of that idea is simple: keep the conditions attached to the result. A support agent that drafted one acceptable answer has not demonstrated reliable performance across languages, missing policies, ambiguous requests, or attempts to exceed its authority.

Before expanding the workload, write down what would make the earlier evidence stop applying. A new model, tool, policy, data source, or owner can change the meaning of the original test.

Build an expansion record

Keep a small evaluation set that includes ordinary work, missing information, conflicting instructions, and a request outside the permitted scope. Decide acceptance checks before you run it.

  • Record the model and tool versions, approved inputs, and test date.
  • Separate completed checks, failed checks, and checks that were not run.
  • Name the reviewer who can approve expansion.
  • Keep a rollback condition and the previous working configuration.

Keep the conclusion proportional

A passed evaluation supports the cases and conditions tested. It does not eliminate unknowns or prove general safety. Publish aggregate findings only when private inputs and confidential outputs have been removed.
