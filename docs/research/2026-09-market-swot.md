# Arctura market SWOT and attention thesis

Updated: 2026-09-02

## Decision

Position Arctura as the accountability layer for AI agents, led by a free Agent Accountability Card and followed by the existing Work Order. Do not lead with a broad professional-network claim until member activity supports it.

## Market signals

- Microsoft reports that advanced agent users document workflows, handoffs, and quality standards more often, while organizational readiness is the larger constraint on impact: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization
- PwC reports that skills in AI-exposed jobs are changing more than twice as fast and that judgment and leadership are gaining value: https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/ai/ai-jobs-barometer.html
- Google's A2A protocol uses machine-readable Agent Cards for capability discovery, but a capability description is not an accountability record: https://developers.googleblog.com/en/a2a-a-new-era-of-agent-interoperability/
- A2A moved to Linux Foundation governance with support from more than 100 companies, strengthening the case for interoperable records rather than another closed directory: https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-launches-the-agent2agent-protocol-project-to-enable-secure-intelligent-communication-between-ai-agents
- AWS identifies ownership, review status, discovery, lineage, and audit trails as the bottleneck when organizations scale agents: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/manage-agents-tools-and-skills-at-scale-with-aws-agent-registry/
- The World Economic Forum says most organizations remain unsure how to evaluate and govern agents, despite high planned adoption: https://www.weforum.org/publications/ai-agents-in-action-foundations-for-evaluation-and-governance/
- LinkedIn is moving from self-declared skills toward proficiency signals based on usage and outcomes: https://news.linkedin.com/2026/Professional_Edge_Skills_Verified

## SWOT

### Strengths

- Existing coherent primitives: Work Standard, Work Order, public evidence, authority boundaries, profiles, and validator.
- Local-first tools reduce privacy friction and produce inspectable artifacts immediately.
- Evidence-bounded voice is differentiated from hype-heavy agent marketplaces.

### Weaknesses

- The broad “professional network for the agentic age” promise runs ahead of two public profiles and inactive accounts.
- Multiple concepts compete for the homepage's attention: network, Bittensor implementation, governance lens, content, and tools.
- No single artifact is yet named in the language of the market's immediate pain: agent inventory, ownership, authority, handoff, and review.

### Opportunities

- Own the missing human accountability companion to protocol-level capability cards.
- Earn high-intent attention through a free tool, open schema, examples, and machine-readable exports rather than opinion content alone.
- Turn every generated card into the start of a Work Order and, later, a proof-backed profile record.
- Publish accountable-agent examples for support, research, coding, finance, and operations as useful long-tail entry points.

### Threats

- Cloud registries can absorb governance metadata into existing enterprise platforms.
- LinkedIn and hiring platforms can add agent-operated work and verified-output fields to profiles.
- “Agent passport” and reputation language is crowded, speculative, and vulnerable to vague certification claims.
- An unpopulated directory can signal absence rather than quality.

## Attention thesis

The highest-quality attention will come from builders and operators searching for practical agent governance, not from a generic social-network launch. The acquisition unit should be a usable artifact: build a card, export it, attach it to a repository or agent profile, then define the first task with a Work Order.

Near-term distribution should prioritize:

1. An open schema and worked examples on GitHub.
2. Search pages that solve one concrete job, such as an AI agent governance checklist or customer-support agent card.
3. Short visual teardowns showing why a capability claim is incomplete without owner, limits, handoffs, and checks.
4. Direct practitioner outreach asking for critique of the schema, with published revisions and explicit attribution.

Success is not raw traffic. Measure completed card exports, card-to-Work-Order progression, referenced cards in external repositories, and source-backed profiles created from real work.
