Keeping the Record True

Records go stale. How Arctura rechecks claims over time and corrects the public record in the open instead of editing quietly.

Status: Published methodology, version 0.1. This defines a review standard; it does not claim every described capability is deployed.

Home / The Work Standard / Keeping the Record True

Records go stale. Outcomes get checked again after the fact. When something turns out to be wrong, it's corrected on the record — not quietly edited, not left to rot.

Why records go stale even when nothing was done wrong

A claim can be accurate the day it's published and wrong six months later — conditions change, a result doesn't hold up over time, an outcome that looked final gets reversed. This isn't a failure of the original work. It's a normal thing that happens to any record over time, and the standard exists to catch it, not to prevent it.

What maintenance actually requires

  • Rechecking claims on a schedule, not just when someone happens to notice a problem.
  • Correcting in place, with a visible trail. When a claim turns out to be wrong, the correction is added and dated. The original claim isn't deleted — the record shows what was said, what was found, and when it changed.
  • Treating silence as a risk. A page that hasn't been checked in a long time isn't automatically wrong, but it's automatically due for a look.

What this rules out

  • Quiet edits. Changing a claim without leaving a trace of what it used to say erases the accountability the record exists to provide.
  • "Set and forget" publishing. Treating a published page as finished, with no plan to ever check it again.
  • Deleting instead of correcting. Removing a wrong claim entirely, rather than showing it was wrong and what replaced it, hides the mistake instead of accounting for it.

Why this matters

A record that's accurate once and never checked again slowly becomes fiction without anyone deciding to make it so. Maintenance is what keeps the record's age from becoming a liability.

How it connects

This closes the loop with Part 5: Proof Over Trust — a record only stays worth checking if it's kept accurate over time, not just at the moment it was published.
