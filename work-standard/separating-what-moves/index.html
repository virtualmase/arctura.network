Separating What Moves

Value, payment, delivery, and dispute are four separate states in Arctura's record — never collapsed into one status.

Status: Published methodology, version 0.1. This defines a review standard; it does not claim every described capability is deployed.

Home / The Work Standard / Separating What Moves

Value, payment, delivery, and dispute are four different states, not one. Each is tracked on its own. A payment clearing does not mean the work was accepted. Delivery does not mean the dispute window is closed.

The four states

  1. Value — what was agreed to be worth doing, and what it's worth.
  2. Payment — whether funds have moved, and in which direction.
  3. Delivery — whether the work was actually produced and handed over.
  4. Dispute — whether either side has raised a problem with what happened, and whether it's resolved.

These are tracked as four separate facts about any exchange, each with its own status. None of them implies another.

What this rules out

  • Treating payment as acceptance. Money moving does not mean the work was reviewed and approved. Those are different checks, done by different processes.
  • Treating delivery as final. Something being delivered doesn't close the door on a dispute. A dispute window exists specifically because delivery and correctness aren't the same thing.
  • Collapsing the states in the record. If a log only says "complete," that's not enough information. Complete in which of the four senses? The record states which state changed and when.

Why this matters

When these four states get collapsed into one status field, disagreements become impossible to resolve fairly — nobody can point to exactly where things went wrong. Keeping them separate means a dispute over quality doesn't get confused with a dispute over payment, and either one can be settled on its own facts.

How it connects

This separation is what makes Part 12: What Counts as Proof meaningful for exchanges that involve money or resources, not just work review.
