Guide / Human review

When an AI decision affects you, start here.

Five practical questions for people and citizens who want to understand an automated result and ask the responsible operator for review.

Describe what happened

Start with the result you received: a message, recommendation, account action, or other decision. Save the date and any reference number. You do not need to determine which model was used before asking who is responsible.

Keep your own private record. When contacting a service, use its established support channel. Avoid publishing identity documents, account credentials, private conversations, or information about someone else.

1. Who is responsible for the outcome?

Ask which person or organization operates the system and which team can review the result. A model name or vendor logo is not the same as an accountable operator.

A useful answer identifies a responsible role and a reachable review channel. If the system can only repeat its answer, ask the service operating it for human review.

2. What was the system allowed to do?

Ask whether the system produced a suggestion, prepared an action for approval, or acted directly. These are different responsibilities.

For example, a support assistant might draft a response for a staff member. That does not mean it can change an account or make a financial commitment. Ask about the actual action that affected you.

3. What information and checks supported the result?

Ask for the relevant inputs, applicable policy, and checks in a form the operator can share safely. You may not receive internal prompts or another person’s data; the useful request is an explanation of your result and its basis.

If the information is wrong or incomplete, identify the specific correction. “The address used is outdated” gives a reviewer more to investigate than a general objection to AI.

4. Can a person review and correct it?

Ask for the route to human review, what information is needed, and how you will be told the outcome. Keep the request focused on the disputed result.

Arctura’s sample request: “Please identify the team responsible for this result. I believe the following input is incorrect: ____. Please explain the review process and confirm whether a person can reconsider the outcome.”

5. What should the corrected record show?

Keep the original result, the correction you requested, and the final response. If a correction is made, ask whether it changes related decisions or records.

This guide offers practical questions, not a statement of legal rights or a guarantee of access to an appeal. Arctura does not run an appeals service for outside organizations. For a worked illustration of responsibility and handoff, open our fictional support-review example.
