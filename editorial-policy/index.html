Our working principles

Inspect the records ↗

Start with a useful question

We prioritize agent authority, evaluation, human review, interoperability, and public evidence. Search demand and industry attention can surface questions; they do not establish facts or justify repetitive pages.

Keep the source and interpretation distinct

Briefings link primary sources where available and identify our interpretation. Source checks carry a date. An event’s date, a source’s publication date, and our analysis date are separate facts.

We do not present a source citation as an endorsement. We do not invent mentions, adoption statistics, citation counts, or results. Historical references remain labeled as such.

Label the kind of evidence

Methodology describes a process. A template is an empty record. An illustrative example demonstrates a structure. An observed evaluation documents a run. A testnet record does not establish production operation.

Use automation with a review boundary

Automated collection can identify candidate sources and changes. AI can assist research and drafting. Candidate records are not published articles: publication requires source checks, an original useful explanation, and review of the claims.

Our public feed contains published editorial records only. Collection timestamps do not refresh article dates. The opening collection is curated analysis, not a claim of continuous monitoring.

Correct the record

Open a correction with the page URL, the disputed statement, and the supporting public source. Do not include private customer material, credentials, or confidential logs.

For a material correction, update the explanation, modification date, and correction note. Minor formatting changes do not imply a new source review.

Respect privacy and context

Publish only material that can be shared appropriately. Preserve limitations when summarizing. For tool-specific handling of local drafts and analytics, read the privacy explanation on the tool itself. This policy does not grant permission to republish someone else’s work.
