# 12. What Counts as Proof

Evidence that supports a claim is assembled so it can be independently checked — logs, records, outcomes. A single example is not a guarantee, and is never presented as one.

## What a proof packet contains

- **The specific claim being supported.** Stated precisely enough that it's clear what would count as contradicting it.
- **The underlying record.** Commit hashes, logs, dated entries, reviewer names — whatever a third party would need to check the claim without asking the network to vouch for it.
- **The scope of what it proves.** One documented outcome proves that outcome happened once, under those conditions. It does not prove the outcome is typical, guaranteed, or will happen again.

## What this rules out

- **One example dressed up as a pattern.** A single Field Notes entry showing a good result is evidence of that one result. It is not evidence of a trend unless multiple entries are cited and the pattern is shown, not asserted.
- **Curated evidence.** Presenting only the outcomes that support a claim while omitting contradicting ones. If the record has failures alongside successes, a proof packet acknowledges that the full record exists, even when citing a specific success.
- **Evidence that can't actually be checked.** A screenshot with no source, a quote with no attribution, a number with no method behind it. If a third party can't verify it, it doesn't count as proof — it counts as an assertion.

## Why this matters

The gap between "this happened once" and "this is what you should expect" is where most overstated claims live. Proof packets exist to keep that gap visible instead of letting a single good result imply more than it actually shows.

## How it connects

This is the standard behind every citation in [Evidence](/evidence/) and every claim made anywhere else on the network — see [Part 2: What a Claim Needs](/work-standard/what-a-claim-needs/) for how the claim itself gets worded before it's backed by a packet like this.
