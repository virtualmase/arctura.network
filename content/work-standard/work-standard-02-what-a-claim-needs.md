# 2. What a Claim Needs

Every claim made on the record separates fact from interpretation. Nothing is stated as settled that isn't.

## The three kinds of statement

1. **Fact.** Something that happened and can be checked independently — a commit was made, a review was completed, a value was recorded. Facts get cited with a link to the evidence.
2. **Interpretation.** A conclusion drawn from facts — "this suggests demand is growing," "this pattern looks like a bottleneck." Interpretations are labeled as interpretations, not stated as fact.
3. **Promise.** A commitment about the future — "this will scale," "this will always be available." Promises are the rarest and riskiest kind of claim. They're only made when the network can actually back them.

Mixing these together is the most common way a record becomes untrustworthy. A claim that blends fact and interpretation without marking the seam reads as more certain than it is.

## The rule

If a claim can't be traced back to a fact, it doesn't go on the record as one. It either gets labeled as interpretation, or it doesn't get published yet.

This applies everywhere — Field Notes, documentation, the site itself. "This is fast" is an interpretation unless a number backs it. "This passed review on [date]" is a fact if the review record exists.

## Why this matters

A network that mixes fact and opinion freely trains people to stop checking anything, because they can't tell which claims are solid. Keeping the seam visible is what makes it worth checking in the first place.

## How it connects

This is the standard every entry in [Field Notes](/insights/) and [Evidence](/evidence/) is held to. See [Part 12: What Counts as Proof](/work-standard/what-counts-as-proof/) for how claims get backed once they're made.
