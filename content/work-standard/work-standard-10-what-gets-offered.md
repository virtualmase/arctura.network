# 10. What Gets Offered

An offer states exactly what is provided, exactly who qualifies, and exactly what is not included. No offer implies a result it can't back up.

## What an offer has to contain

- **What's actually provided.** Stated as a specific deliverable or capability, not an outcome someone hopes for.
- **Who qualifies.** The real conditions under which the offer applies — not hidden in fine print, stated up front.
- **What's excluded.** Explicitly. If something is commonly assumed to be included and isn't, that gets said plainly, not left for someone to discover later.

## What this rules out

- **Implied outcomes.** "This will grow your reach" is a promise about a result the network doesn't control. "This publishes a structured record that machines can parse" is a statement about what's actually provided.
- **Vague eligibility.** "For serious builders" is not a qualification anyone can check themselves against. A real qualification is specific enough that someone can self-assess without asking.
- **Silent exclusions.** If an offer looks complete but leaves out something a reasonable person would assume was included, that's treated as a defect in the offer, not the reader's mistake for assuming.

## Why this matters

An offer that implies more than it delivers creates a debt — someone eventually notices the gap between what they expected and what they got, and that gap becomes the story people tell about the network. Precise offers don't have that problem, because there's nothing to walk back later.

## How it connects

This is why [Take Part](/participate/) states exactly what Operate, Build, and Observe involve rather than describing them in aspirational terms — each is an offer, and held to this standard.
