# 3. One Record Per Thing

Every person, tool, and process that acts in the network has exactly one canonical record. No duplicate identities, no conflicting descriptions.

## What a canonical record is

One page, one entry, one source of truth for who or what something is. If a contributor, a tool, or a process is described in more than one place, those descriptions have to agree — and ideally, only one of them is authoritative and the rest link back to it.

A canonical record states:

- What the thing is (in plain terms)
- What it's authorized to do
- Who is accountable for it
- Where its history is recorded

## What this rules out

- **Duplicate profiles.** The same contributor described differently in two places. Pick one, link the other to it.
- **Silent forks.** A tool or process that gets modified without the canonical record being updated to match. If the record doesn't reflect reality, it isn't canonical anymore.
- **Anonymous authority.** Something that acts in the network without a record explaining what it is and who's accountable for it doesn't get to act.

## Why this matters

When two records disagree about who or what something is, nobody can tell which one to trust — and the honest answer is neither, until it's fixed. One record per thing means anyone checking the network always knows where to look.

## How it connects

Canonical records are what [Part 4: Before an Agent Acts](/work-standard/before-an-agent-acts/) checks against before authorizing any action, and what [Authority](/authority/) draws its accountability from.
